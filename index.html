Read the estimate carefully

Why show a range?

A prompt does not go to a known building. The model, data-center cooling system, local weather, and electricity supply can all change the result. A single confident number would hide those unknowns.

01

One response is not a token count

This calculator counts generated replies. It does not measure tokens, prompt length, or a particular model's workload. Longer or more complex requests can require different amounts of computing.

02

The timing control is a what-if

The study says water efficiency varies with location and time. The cooler and hotter settings use the endpoints of its modeled on-site cooling range. They do not tell us where or when a commercial model ran.

03

The lawn comparison is a scale reference

Sprinkler time assumes a 2-gallon-per-minute flow. Lawn area estimates how much space that water would cover at one inch deep. Actual sprinklers and watering practices vary.

Sources & assumptions

This calculator is a research-based illustration, not a measurement of any specific chatbot.

  1. Li, P., Yang, J., Islam, M. A. & Ren, S. (2023). “Making AI Less ‘Thirsty’: Uncovering and Addressing the Secret Water Footprint of AI Models.” The paper's GPT-3 example uses 3.96 Wh per response, a power usage effectiveness (PUE) of 1.2, electricity water intensity of 1.8 L/kWh, and on-site cooling water effectiveness (WUE) from 0.5 to 5 L/kWh. Applying its formula gives about 10.5–28.4 mL per response.
  2. Lawn conversions. Sprinkler flow is set to 2 US gallons per minute as an example assumption. One US gallon spread over one square foot is about 1.604 inches deep, so one inch over one square foot is about 0.623 gallons.
Limits: The per-response estimate reflects an older, specific research example, not a universal average for today's LLMs. Providers do not publish enough request-level operational data to verify the water used by an individual answer. The cooler/hotter comparison changes the study's on-site cooling assumption while holding its other inputs constant. It illustrates uncertainty; it does not predict real time-of-day savings.
